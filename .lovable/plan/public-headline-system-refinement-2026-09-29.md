# Public headline system refinement

## Goal
Make every headline on GDPVision’s public editorial journey immediately clear to a senior government reader: what the page or section is about, why it matters, and what the reader should do or understand next.

The pass will preserve the current page structure, visual design, factual claims, citations, safeguards, calculations, and overall narrative flow.

## Scope
Included:
- Homepage, including the Sovereignty section and all ten Chamber panels
- The Sovereign Vault
- The Business Case
- The Decision Brief and public calculator
- The Writing index and each public article
- Shared public navigation, cross-page prompts, and briefing calls to action
- Page titles and social descriptions where they must align with revised visible headlines

Excluded:
- Signed-in, admin, console, and Chamber workspaces
- Private or tokenized shared documents
- Sign-in, invitation, password-reset, and kiosk screens
- Generated article body copy, calculations, claims, citations, and legal/security meaning

## Recommended headline standard
Use one clear hierarchy throughout:

```text
Eyebrow: names the subject or stage
Headline: states one direct promise, decision, outcome, or finding
Supporting line: explains how or why in plain executive English
Action: names the exact next step and destination
```

Headline rules:
- Lead with the decision or result, not a product metaphor.
- Prefer a complete, active statement over a string of abstract nouns.
- Reserve questions for genuine decision pivots, not routine section introductions.
- Keep one idea per headline; move qualifications into the supporting line.
- Name Cabinet, government, or the nation when the audience would otherwise be unclear.
- Use the same term for the same action everywhere.
- Preserve exact security, evidence, procurement, and economic qualifications beneath the headline.

## Planned changes

### 1. Establish the first-page promise
- Replace the homepage’s metaphor-led opening with a direct statement about testing a national economic decision before committing to it.
- Keep “rehearse” as distinctive GDPVision language, but define it immediately in the supporting line rather than requiring readers to discover its meaning later.
- Align the opening, rotating Cabinet question, and first action so they form one guided sequence: decision → test → briefing.

### 2. Make every homepage section advance the argument
- Rewrite each major section headline so a headline-only scan tells a coherent story from national risk to evidence, rehearsal, delivery, control, proof, and engagement.
- Retain questions only where they deliberately reframe the reader’s decision, such as evidence confidence.
- Give each regional-exposure headline a clear decision implication instead of leaving it as an alarm statement.
- Replace compressed or abstract headings with one direct proposition.

### 3. Strengthen the ten Chamber outcomes
- Audit every Chamber image caption and section heading against the same outcome standard.
- Replace vague activities such as “manage,” “empower,” and “conduct” with visible government outcomes.
- Keep each Chamber distinct while making the ten outcomes read as one aligned national operating system.
- Review shared Chamber wording wherever it also appears in public articles so one edit does not create contradictory copy.

### 4. Align the Business Case and Decision Brief
- Preserve the Business Case’s numbered, evidence-led structure, which is already the strongest headline hierarchy.
- Convert its isolated question-style section into a direct statement consistent with the rest of the paper.
- Make the Decision Brief headline state what the tool helps government estimate, while retaining the public-estimate and non-predictive qualifications.
- Distinguish the Business Case, Decision Brief, and Writing links by the value each destination provides rather than repeating generic “read” labels.

### 5. Clarify the Sovereign Vault promise
- Simplify the Vault’s two-clause opening into one immediately understood promise: sensitive national data remains inside government control while still informing decisions.
- Keep all existing claims about custody, encryption, approvals, disconnected operation, and controlled outputs exact.
- Align the visible headline, page title, and social description around the same promise.

### 6. Give public writing a proper editorial hierarchy
- Give the Writing index a true page-level headline and retain article titles as the primary headline on each article.
- Review all public article title/standfirst pairs for directness and alignment without rewriting article arguments or sourced claims.
- Organize the index closing prompts into a clear priority: continue reading, understand the case, or request a briefing.

### 7. Standardize guidance and conversion language
- Use one canonical briefing action across navigation, page endings, and the shared form: **Request a Cabinet briefing**.
- Use destination-specific supporting labels, such as understanding the procurement case or reading the arguments, rather than generic repeated links.
- Ensure every major public page closes with one obvious next step and no competing headline language.

### 8. Validate the complete public journey
- Check every included page on desktop and mobile for heading hierarchy, line wrapping, clipping, and visual balance.
- Verify each page has one clear page headline followed by correctly ordered section headings.
- Confirm revised page titles, descriptions, and social metadata match the visible promise.
- Run focused code checks and inspect the live public journey for errors or broken links.

## Success criteria
- A reader scanning only headlines can explain what GDPVision does, why it matters, how government stays in control, and what to do next.
- Every headline carries one clear idea and supports the section immediately below it.
- The ten Chambers use parallel, outcome-led language.
- The primary briefing action has one name across all public editorial pages.
- No factual claim, calculation, citation, security safeguard, page structure, or private/shared document changes.
