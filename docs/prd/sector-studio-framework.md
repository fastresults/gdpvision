# Sector Studio (chamber 10) — the method

Bundled into every Sector Studio context pack as `method.*` lines, so plans are drafted to the same method. Edit this file to change the method; the change is a reviewed commit and reaches the next draft.

Thesis: **pick few, commit long, run it weekly, measure it publicly.**

## Evidence

Nations that grew a sector deliberately — Malaysia (Economic Transformation Programme, 12 National Key Economic Areas, 8-week labs with 425 people from 211 companies, 131 Entry Point Projects, a delivery unit in the Prime Minister's Department), Singapore (23 Industry Transformation Maps covering about 80% of GDP, the Future Economy Council, tripartite councils with four pillars: productivity, jobs and skills, innovation, internationalisation), Costa Rica (COMEX, CINDE and PROCOMER; an investor index scoring jobs, wages and linkages; medical devices about 30% of exports by 2023), Ireland (IDA, a professional sector-specialised agency with decades of continuity), Rwanda (annual public performance contracts between the President and those who deliver, quarterly reporting, public ranking) and South Korea (export targets as the condition for support) — shared seven ingredients. Recent economics (Juhász, Lane and Rodrik, 2023) finds targeted sector policy works when it is disciplined, and that the ability to let losers go matters more than perfect picking.

## Seven ingredients

1. A visible chooser at the top who names the sectors and reviews them for a decade.
2. Few sectors, chosen on evidence, with a written exit rule.
3. One accountable minister and one professional agency.
4. A standing public-private council with a charter.
5. A plan written as projects with owners, dates, funding and KPIs — built in labs, chased by a delivery unit.
6. Conditions on support and public scorecards.
7. A skills pipeline and a national narrative.

Small island states: two to four sectors; lean, partly private machinery; the diaspora as talent and capital; a resilience clause in every pillar.

## Head of Government

Role: choose the sectors; appoint the minister, the Council's industry deputy chair and the delivery lead; sign a Sector Compact with the minister; chair a quarterly stocktake (monthly in year one); unblock what only the top can unblock; tell the nation. Toolkit: sector shortlist brief; Sector Compact (one page, signed, published); stocktake pack; unblock ledger; a delivery function of three to six people in the Office; an annual public report. Test: the Head of Government can say, without notes, the three targets, who owns them, and when the next review is.

## Line minister

Role: owns the number; signs and reports against the Compact; chairs the Sector Council monthly; runs a weekly ministry delivery meeting; brokers blockers across government; fronts the sector to investors and the public. Toolkit: living ministerial brief; scorecard (outcome, delivery, enabler KPIs, traffic-lit, every red with an owner and a date); project register; Cabinet paper generator; investor and partner pack; fixed delivery rhythm; skills and talent tracker. Resist: the Council as a grievance forum, projects added faster than they close, the scorecard reported by the people it measures.

## Sector Council

A standing, chartered, tripartite body that runs the plan between stocktakes; not advisory. Seats (12 to 16): minister as chair; industry deputy chair appointed by the Head of Government; five or six firm leaders across the value chain including an SME and a new entrant; labour; education and training; the investment and export agency; finance; the regulator or utility that gates the sector; the diaspora or civil society; the delivery lead as non-voting secretary. Mandate: adopt and revise the plan; approve, sequence and retire projects; own and publish the scorecard quarterly; resolve blockers or escalate within 30 days; commission research; set skills pipeline targets. Toolkit: charter; monthly meeting pack; decision log; three to five working groups with 90-day workplans; the lab method (four to eight weeks, 30 to 60 people, ending in signed project charters); a research desk; a public register. Guardrails: fixed, staggered three-year terms; published conflicts of interest; abstention when a member's firm stands to benefit; an annual independent review of the scorecard's data.

## Implementation plan

Six stages, each needing named research before it is written. (1) Diagnostic — growth diagnostics, economic complexity and product-space adjacency, value-chain map, three-peer benchmark, firm census, cost and regulatory audits; output: the binding constraint, evidenced. (2) Ambition and choice — demand outlook, 20 to 40 investor soundings, asset inventory; output: thesis, targets at 3, 5 and 10 years, segments in and out, exit rule. (3) Strategy pillars — market access, skills gap, finance, enablers, resilience; output: five to seven pillars with owners. (4) Entry Point Projects — lab with industry, business cases, land and permit checks; output: 8 to 20 charters and a flagship. (5) Enablers and policy — legal drafting, incentives with conditions and sunset, institutions, three-year budget lines. (6) Measurement and review — baselines from the ledger and standards audit, data-collection plan, independent review; output: scorecard, calendar, pivot triggers.

Rules: no KPI without baseline, source and owner (otherwise it goes on the data-collection plan); support carries conditions and a sunset; a project that misses two milestones goes to the Council to be retired or rescued; the plan is versioned and approved by two people; every pillar has a resilience clause.

## National sensitisation

A decade of work to make the sector part of how the nation sees itself. Four audiences: citizens and voters; students, parents and job-seekers; firms and workers in the sector; investors, buyers and the diaspora — each with what they must come to believe, channels and a measure. Toolkit: a one-page sector narrative in the national voice with three numbers everyone should know; brand and message architecture under the national (flag-led) brand; an annual calendar; an education pipeline plan; the public scorecard; a feedback loop (surveys and public comment); a speaking kit for ministers and MPs. Only approved scorecard numbers are used in public. Test: a schoolteacher in the smallest parish can name the sector and one reason it matters.

## Orchestration

Agents, one call per stage: Scout (shortlist) → Diagnostician → Strategist → Planner → Economist → Measurer → Drafter → Auditor. People decide at three points: which sectors (the Head of Government), what the lab confirms, and approval (two people). Context packs are cited lines with a hash; a changed pack marks a section out of date and blocks approval. Auditor gates: citation coverage, every KPI with baseline, source and owner, every project with owner, funder, date and KPI, targets identical across ambition, Compact and scorecard, no unapproved investment data, no personal data.
