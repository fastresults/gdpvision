# Plain-language refinement for the core public journey

## Objective
Make GDPVision easier to understand for ministers, senior officials, journalists, and educated general readers while preserving its authority, evidence, visual structure, and consulting-conversion flow.

## Recommendation
Use **clear executive English** throughout: direct, dignified, and specific. The public pages should explain the government outcome first, the practical method second, and specialist detail only where it builds trust.

The current structure is strong and should remain. The revision should reduce abstraction rather than reduce substance.

### Writing standard
- Put the consequence or benefit before the mechanism.
- Keep one main idea per sentence and shorten long multi-clause passages.
- Prefer familiar government language over product or engineering language.
- Replace specialist terms when an everyday equivalent is accurate.
- Where a formal term matters, explain it on first use in plain English.
- Prefer active subjects: “Cabinet can compare…” rather than “options are appraised…”.
- Preserve every sourced figure, confidence grade, caution, security qualification, and approval safeguard.
- Avoid becoming casual, promotional, absolute, or simplistic.

### Plain-language translation pattern
| Current style | Recommended direction |
|---|---|
| “instrumentation” | “the information and tools government needs to decide” |
| “sovereign instance” | “a separate system for each country” |
| “corpus” | “national evidence record” |
| “decision surface” | “one clear view for a decision” |
| “provenance” | “source and history” |
| “deterministic engine” | “a calculation that gives the same result from the same inputs” |
| “basis points of GDP” | “share of GDP,” with the technical measure retained only beside detailed figures |
| “glide-path” | “planned transition over time” |
| “ontology” | “shared structure for organising national data” |
| “artefact” | “record,” “brief,” or “document,” depending on context |
| “conversion criteria” | “the agreed test for moving from pilot to national use” |

## Scope and sequence

### 1. Homepage first
Revise the homepage from top to bottom while keeping every section, image, interaction, and call to action in place.

- Preserve the central promise: rehearse important national decisions before committing.
- Simplify the rotating Cabinet questions and responses.
- Clarify the national evidence, decision journey, first engagement, ten chambers, Counsel, sovereignty, proof, and briefing sections.
- Keep the ten outcome captions concise and action-led; simplify chamber purposes and proof points where needed.
- Replace internal product language with the result a government reader will recognise.
- Retain the existing dignified voice and evidence discipline.

### 2. Instrument and sovereignty language
Treat these homepage sections as the bridge between the promise and the specialist pages.

- Explain “the Instrument” as a working national decision system before using the product label alone.
- Explain sovereignty through practical questions: where records stay, who can see them, who approves release, and what government keeps.
- Replace technical access and deployment language with clear operational assurances without weakening their meaning.
- Keep the route into the Vault and Business Case clear and consistent.

### 3. Sovereign Vault
Keep the current page order and three-rule explanation.

- Lead with the practical assurance: sensitive government records remain inside government premises.
- Translate terms such as “cluster,” “aggregate,” “mutually authenticated channel,” “catalogue,” and “release queue,” while retaining necessary security precision.
- Keep tax, customs, treasury, registry, health, crime, and employment records in the opening example.
- Make each security statement understandable to a non-technical Cabinet reader and still credible to a technical reviewer.
- Preserve the three-unit image, briefing flow, and qualification that specifications vary by deployment.

### 4. Business Case
This page needs the deepest edit because its argument is strong but contains the highest concentration of specialist language and long sentences.

- Preserve the decision-paper structure, numbered sections, evidence, sources, and options appraisal.
- Rewrite the executive summary so a reader can grasp the decision, urgency, alternatives, and recommendation in one pass.
- Replace engineering and software vocabulary in the main argument with government consequences.
- Keep technical distinctions where they establish procurement, security, reproducibility, and accountability—but explain them plainly.
- Simplify the ten chamber descriptions, three procurement paths, five approval perspectives, risks, and recommended path.
- Preserve every caveat: no unsupported outcomes, no invented savings, and no weakening of the distinction between an open model and an authorised government capability.

### 5. Decision Brief and calculator
Simplify all visible guidance while leaving calculations and controls unchanged.

- Clarify what is drawn from public records, what is a reference assumption, and what the visitor changed.
- Replace labels such as “modelled uplift,” “stance,” “attribution,” and “addressable loss” where a clearer phrase is available.
- Explain the GDP cap, return, payback, evidence status, chamber sequence, and downloadable justification in direct language.
- Simplify the AI counsel, evidence pathway, form, confirmation, and printable brief copy.
- Keep every figure interrogable through the existing explanations and preserve the statement that the tool frames a decision rather than predicts the future.

### 6. Shared public language
Align the copy used across the journey so the same idea has the same name everywhere.

- Navigation, footer, page metadata, briefing forms, confirmations, shared chamber descriptions, exposure slides, and calls to action.
- Use one consistent vocabulary for national evidence, government-held records, assumptions, approvals, the Vault, the Decision Brief, and the national briefing.
- Keep opinion essays and token-based shared documents outside this first pass.

## Quality controls
- Compare every revised factual statement against the original before replacement.
- Do not alter citations, grades, numbers, legal meaning, security claims, or procurement qualifications.
- Do not add testimonials, outcomes, delivery timelines, guarantees, or claims not already supported.
- Check reading flow at desktop and phone widths without redesigning the pages.
- Verify all public links, Vault and national briefing submissions, Decision Brief controls, downloadable output, and metadata.
- Review the final copy for consistent Caribbean and government terminology.

## Technical implementation
- Copy-only changes in the public route, shared marketing, chamber-content, business-case, calculator, briefing, and print-copy sources.
- Preserve layouts, styling, illustrations, screenshots, animations, calculations, server behaviour, and database structures.
- Keep canonical shared copy in its existing central sources so homepage, Business Case, and other public surfaces do not drift.

## Completion standard
A reader should be able to answer these questions without interpreting product language:
1. What problem does GDPVision solve for government?
2. What does it help Cabinet do before, during, and after a decision?
3. What information does it use, and how is that information protected?
4. What is the difference between the public calculator and an engaged government deployment?
5. What is the next practical step?
