# Rebuild all six Chamber journey phases as one detailed visual decision sequence

## Recommendation

Replace the current succession of mostly static cards with a single **Sovereign Decision Theatre**. The visual language remains GDPVision’s restrained graphite-and-gold instrument style, but every phase becomes a short, legible process with its own sub-steps, moving evidence, visible state changes, and a concrete handoff into the next phase.

The screenshots confirm that the greatest gap is in phases 01–03: they currently present a quotation, one framed statement, and three generic evidence cards. Phases 04–05 carry more operational depth, but their motion still reads primarily as elements appearing. The redesign will make all six phases equally specific to the selected scenario and visually connected from beginning to end.

## Continuous visual grammar

- A **decision docket** enters in phase 01 and remains the same identifiable object through all six phases.
- Evidence is represented by small engraved documents and signal marks, not generic decorative dots.
- A thin gold route visibly carries the docket, approved evidence, and resulting decision between work areas.
- Each sub-step has a short action label, a visible operation, and a resulting artefact; the viewer always sees what changed.
- Completed work accumulates in a persistent miniature **decision file**, so phases do not feel disconnected.
- Motion communicates causality: documents travel, gates open, scores settle, findings are stamped, and responsibility is assigned. Avoid ornamental movement without meaning.
- Clicking anywhere in the animation toggles pause/resume from the current point. The existing Play, Previous, Next, Replay, and phase controls remain available.

## Phase 01 — The question: turn an issue into a decision docket

Break this into four beats:

1. **Signal arrives** — the scenario enters as a dated Cabinet note; urgency and journey type are stamped onto it.
2. **Decision is isolated** — key phrases lift from the question and connect to the decision that must be taken.
3. **Consequences spread** — small GDP, fiscal, jobs, credibility, and delivery gauges respond only where relevant to that scenario.
4. **Clock starts** — the ordinary decision time appears beside the GDPVision path, and the docket moves into intake.

The screen ends with a concise line: **“Cabinet must decide: [scenario-specific decision].”**

## Phase 02 — Ask GDPVision: visibly frame the inquiry

Replace the single statement panel with a six-part intake desk:

1. **Country context** — identifies the national baseline and affected population or sector.
2. **Decision authority** — names who can decide and who must advise.
3. **Time horizon** — distinguishes immediate response, budget-cycle action, and long-term consequence.
4. **Economic exposure** — opens the relevant GDP, fiscal, foreign-exchange, jobs, service, or credibility lenses.
5. **Constraints** — adds legal, budget, capacity, political, and sovereign-data boundaries where relevant.
6. **Decision test** — rewrites the original question into a precise choice with measurable success conditions.

Each field should be populated visibly from the original docket. Connector lines converge into a final **framed inquiry**, which becomes the input to evidence retrieval.

## Phase 03 — Governed evidence: show retrieval, protection, and assurance

Replace the three cards with an animated evidence assembly line:

1. **Search the national record** — scenario-specific public sources emerge from the Corpus.
2. **Open protected evidence** — approved national records illuminate inside a clearly bounded Sovereign Vault; protected records never cross the boundary.
3. **Match and reconcile** — related figures align while conflicting or stale evidence is visibly flagged.
4. **Cite and grade** — every accepted item receives source, date, custody, and quality marks; grades are labelled illustrative.
5. **Approve for use** — only approved findings leave the assurance gate and enter the Chamber routing step.

Motion should include document travel, a locked/private branch, citation threads, reconciliation marks, and an approval gate. The concluding counter should state how many public sources, in-country sources, and flagged gaps are entering the decision.

## Phase 04 — Select the Chamber: make routing an auditable judgement

Deepen the existing three-beat router into five beats:

1. **Extract decision signals** from the framed inquiry.
2. **Test every Chamber** against the decision type, evidence needed, authority, urgency, and intended output.
3. **Show eliminations** with a brief, specific reason rather than simply fading low-fit Chambers.
4. **Identify supporting Chambers** and draw the exact contribution each will provide.
5. **Authorise the lead Chamber** with a routing note showing lead, support, rationale, and required output.

The ten Chambers should behave like a live switchboard: signal lines arrive, fit bars settle, unsuitable routes close, supporting routes remain linked, and the lead Chamber physically opens into phase 05. Fit remains explicitly illustrative and explainable.

## Phase 05 — Open the Chamber: show the Chamber doing the work

Keep each Chamber’s own five-stage method, but turn every stage into a mini-sequence rather than a text change:

1. **Question** — the stage states the exact issue it must resolve.
2. **Inputs called** — only the evidence required for that stage moves from the tray onto the workbench.
3. **Method applied** — a Chamber-specific visual operation occurs: compare, model, map, test, rank, reconcile, simulate, or assign.
4. **Finding produced** — the result appears as a concise, scenario-specific finding rather than a generic restatement.
5. **Safeguard checked** — citation, privacy, range, mandate, approval, or ownership checks visibly pass or flag an exception.
6. **Principal signs off** — the responsible office seals that stage’s output.

The decision file should build line by line as each stage completes. Evidence items visibly move into the active stage; findings then move into the file. The final stage adds the decision, owner, first action, review date, and measurable trigger before the **Decision ready** seal lands.

## Phase 06 — Prime Minister value: show the executive difference, not five claims

Convert the five benefits into a before/after executive dashboard:

1. **Knows** — conflicting evidence resolves into one cited view.
2. **Chooses** — alternatives narrow to the defensible Cabinet choice.
3. **Protects** — exposed risks are bounded by safeguards and contingencies.
4. **Assigns** — ministries and principals resolve into one accountable owner and timetable.
5. **Measures** — indicators connect the decision back to the national record for review.

Each benefit animates from a visible **Before** state into a **With GDPVision** state, using scenario-specific text. The sequence then assembles a single Prime Minister brief containing the decision, why it is defensible, what it protects, who acts next, and what will be measured. End on the qualified GDP pathway: better evidence → faster decision → stronger execution → protected growth.

## Content and data changes

- Expand each journey with a structured decision frame: authority, horizon, exposures, constraints, success test, and scenario-specific evidence gaps.
- Add stage-level inputs, operation, finding, safeguard, sign-off, and resulting file entry for all five stages.
- Keep shared Chamber methods and safeguards where appropriate, but make the question, evidence, finding, and resulting action specific to each of the 50 journeys.
- Replace inferred generic wording where it produces weak or repetitive results; add a development check requiring complete content for every phase and stage.
- Preserve current scenario selection, Chamber filtering, benefit content, illustrative clock, and Explain disclosures.

## Motion and interaction

- Extend the presentation timing so every sub-step is readable; phase duration derives from its number of beats rather than using one fixed timer.
- Previous/Next moves one sub-step at a time while inside a phase, then crosses to the adjacent phase.
- Click the theatre once to pause and again to resume; no hover-based pausing.
- Choosing a phase or scenario resets to its first sub-step and begins the new sequence clearly.
- Use restrained easing, drawn routes, document transfer, gate, stamp, dial, and counter motion; no bouncing or decorative spectacle.
- Reduced-motion mode shows the completed composition for the current phase with every relationship and status still understandable.
- On phones, preserve the same narrative in a vertical sequence with the active work area first; do not shrink the desktop theatre until text becomes unreadable.

## Technical approach

- Split phases 01–03 and 06 into focused journey visual components beside the existing router and dossier.
- Introduce one shared phase/beat controller so autoplay, direct navigation, replay, click-pause, and manual stepping cannot conflict.
- Add reusable visual primitives for the docket, evidence document, route, assurance gate, sign-off seal, decision-file entry, and before/after transformation.
- Add scoped semantic motion styles to the design system and preserve the existing graphite, paper, ink, line, and gold tokens.
- Retain the current route and dialog structure; this is a presentation-layer and scenario-content change only.
