# Homepage copy refinement for consulting conversion

## Objective
Turn the homepage from a sophisticated product explanation into a persuasive path for a President, Prime Minister, Cabinet Secretary, or senior adviser to request a confidential national briefing.

## Recommended message strategy
- Lead with the executive result: better Cabinet decisions, rehearsed against the nation’s own evidence before commitments are made.
- Present GDPVision as the lasting sovereign capability delivered through a high-trust government engagement—not as software competing with consulting.
- Preserve the restrained, statesmanlike voice; remove phrases that feel promotional, absolute, overly technical, or difficult to substantiate.
- Use one conversion journey throughout: recognise the national exposure → see the decision method → trust the safeguards and team → request a country-specific briefing.

## Copy changes

### 1. First screen
- Replace **“Your nation’s GDP growth engine”** with a more credible decision promise, such as **“Rehearse the decisions that will shape your nation’s economy.”**
- Rewrite the opening paragraph around the outcome first, then the mechanism and sovereignty.
- Change the primary action from the generic **“Request a Cabinet briefing”** to a more valuable offer such as **“Request your national decision briefing.”**
- Change the secondary action to **“See how a Cabinet decision moves.”**
- Retain the rotating national threats, but tighten each to a short executive question and concise response so the first screen is easier to scan.

### 2. Establish the engagement before the feature catalogue
- Add a short section before the ten chambers explaining what a government receives:
  1. a confidential national evidence review;
  2. a decision rehearsal using its own data;
  3. a prioritised implementation brief;
  4. a sovereign GDPVision capability that remains with government.
- Make the distinction clear: the engagement does not end with a report; it installs a continuously usable decision discipline.

### 3. Reframe sections around decisions and outcomes
- Change feature-led headings into Cabinet questions: what is exposed, what decision is available, what it costs, who owns delivery, and how progress is judged.
- Shorten the ten chamber descriptions to one outcome sentence and two proof points each.
- Replace internal terminology on first mention—such as corpus, ontology, goal-seek, ingest, snapshots, and diffable—with senior-government language; retain specialist terms only as supporting detail.
- Keep the decision lifecycle as the central explanation, but rewrite it as **Understand → Rehearse → Decide → Deliver → Account** so the commercial value is immediately legible.

### 4. Strengthen proof without overstating it
- Separate verified credentials, working-product proof, and product capability.
- Keep the cited economic evidence and confidence grades as a differentiator.
- Review absolute claims including **“every figure,” “no cross-instance queries exist,” “the government owns it outright,”** and **“one working prototype”** so each is precise and supportable.
- Replace self-awarded language such as **“doctorate-grade”** with observable standards: cited, reviewed, versioned, approved, or traceable.
- Add only approved proof—named engagements, measurable outcomes, authorised testimonials, or demonstrable artifacts. Do not invent customer validation.

### 5. Answer the objections that block a briefing
- Consolidate sovereignty, confidentiality, human approval, deployment, procurement readiness, and transition from existing systems into a concise assurance section.
- Clarify that GDPVision works with the government’s existing records and teams rather than demanding a wholesale replacement on day one.
- State the first engagement’s scope, participants, preparation, output, confidentiality, and next decision without publishing unsupported pricing or timelines.

### 6. Convert the final invitation into a concrete offer
- Rename the section **“Your national decision briefing.”**
- Promise a useful outcome: a country-specific view of one priority decision, the evidence gaps affecting it, and the route to a decision-ready national capability.
- Replace **“Sixty minutes, no slideware”** with an agenda that communicates value, while retaining the dignified, low-pressure tone.
- Reduce the form to the information needed to qualify and prepare the conversation; label the optional context around the national decision currently under consideration.
- Make the confirmation message explain exactly what happens next and who will respond.

## Suggested first-screen copy direction

**Eyebrow**  
GDPVision · A sovereign decision capability

**Headline**  
Rehearse the decisions that will shape your nation’s economy.

**Supporting copy**  
GDPVision gives Presidents, Prime Ministers and Cabinets one trusted view of the national evidence, a disciplined way to test choices before they are taken, and a clear line from decision to delivery. Each country operates in its own sovereign environment, under government control.

**Primary action**  
Request your national decision briefing

**Secondary action**  
See how a Cabinet decision moves

## Technical implementation
- Update homepage, shared chamber, navigation, briefing-form, confirmation, and page-metadata copy only; preserve the existing visual system and interactions.
- Keep canonical chamber descriptions consistent wherever the shared chamber content appears.
- Do not add unverified testimonials, performance claims, client names, delivery promises, or security guarantees.
- Review line lengths and mobile wrapping after copy replacement.

## Validation
- Confirm that the first screen identifies the audience, outcome, differentiation, and next action without scrolling.
- Confirm every section advances one objection or buying decision rather than repeating features.
- Test all briefing links, the form submission journey, confirmation copy, and desktop/mobile readability.
- Check metadata mirrors the revised positioning.
